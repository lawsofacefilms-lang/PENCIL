Why that sentence

It is already the campaign. It is the reason a person with no children in Metro schools should care about a building on the other side of town. This film is built to make that argument land in a room where the ask is large.

What we heard

The reach is the story. So is the range.

159 schools. 81,851 students. 500 square miles. Forty-four years of it.

PENCIL links community resources to Nashville public schools so young people achieve academic success and prepare for life. Time, treasure, and talent, moved through a collective impact model.

That geography is the honest answer to the representation question. Diversity here is not only who is on camera. It is diversity of need, school to school, from one edge of the county to the other. A campaign film that shows the range of the map shows the range of the need without having to say so.

After 44 years, PENCIL is the soft landing place for any adult who wants to get involved with MNPS.

PENCIL Foundation

The work already has a vocabulary: PENCIL Partners, the DG PENCIL Box, Connected Literacy Tutors, Principal for a Day. The film uses PENCIL’s language, not a translation of it.

The brief

What you asked for.

Deliverables
A capital campaign film, 2:30 to 3:00. Social snippets cut from the same material. A b-roll library for landing pages and other campaign uses.
The arc
Who PENCIL is. How much has been done in partnership with Metro Nashville Public Schools. Outgrowing the current space and moving into a new building. The rehab of a former MNPS school, including the building as it stands and the architectural renderings. Testimonials peppered through from leadership, board members and community supporters. Out on a call to action to support the campaign.
The requirement
Cultural awareness across a district this size. Everyone should feel seen and represented. Diversity across Metro Nashville, not only race and background but diversity of need from school to school. A community member with no children in Metro schools should still resonate with it.
The film

A case for support, built as a transformation story.

A school Metro closed, brought back by the community Metro serves. The building carries the narrative. The testimonials are seasoning, not the spine.

This matters for one specific reason. PENCIL already owns a board and community testimonial series on the endowment page. A campaign film that is mostly talking heads is a second version of an asset PENCIL already has. Structuring around the building instead delivers all three things the piece has to accomplish, who PENCIL is, what the MNPS partnership has produced, and why the wider community should care, without stapling three segments together.

Our approach

Film for the campaign. Build the library while we are there.

A shoot day costs the same whether we record five subjects or ten. The crew, the room, the lighting, and the setup are already paid for. We plan around that.

The interview day is the center of it. We build the question set with you first, then record more voices than the campaign film itself needs: leadership, board members, community supporters, partners, parents. The film takes what serves the ask. Everything else is finished material PENCIL already owns.

That surplus is the stockpile. Standalone social cuts, content for landing pages and campaign emails, and, if a second film ever gets funded, the footage to make it without buying another production day at full price.

Same thinking on b-roll. Volunteer events, after-school programs, tutoring sessions, and school appearances are already on PENCIL’s calendar. We schedule against what is happening anyway, so the campaign gets its coverage and PENCIL ends up with a library rather than only the shots this one cut needed.

Interview strategy

Nothing gets captured by accident.

Every question is built backward from something the campaign has to accomplish. Establish that PENCIL can be trusted with the money. Name the constraint. Make the case for the building. Give a donor a reason that is theirs, not PENCIL’s.

We walk into the day knowing which answer serves which moment, and we do not leave until we have it. That is the difference between coming back with footage and coming back with the film. Below is a sample of the set, written for this campaign. The final list is built with you once the subjects are confirmed.

Leadership

  1. You have sat in this district as a teacher, as a parent, and on the board. What do you see from this seat that you could not see from those?
  2. PENCIL covers 159 schools across 500 square miles. What does the range of need actually look like from one end of that map to the other?
  3. What is PENCIL not able to do today because of the space you are in?
  4. Why a building, and why now?

Board and major supporters

  1. What made you say yes to PENCIL the first time?
  2. When you talk about PENCIL to someone who has no children in Metro schools, what do you tell them?
  3. You could put your time and your money almost anywhere in this city. Why here?
  4. What would you want a donor to understand about this campaign before they decide?

Teachers and school partners

  1. Walk me through the last time you shopped the DG PENCIL Box. What did you take, and what would you have done without it?
  2. What does having a PENCIL Partner change about your school year?
  3. What does it cost a teacher, in time or in money, when the resources are not there?

Volunteers and community

  1. What do you actually do when you show up, and what do you get back?
  2. You are not required to be involved in these schools. Why are you?
  3. What would you say to someone in Nashville who thinks this is not their business?

How it runs

What happens after you say yes.

Seven steps. Most of the weight sits on our side, and the two that need PENCIL are the two we flag early.

  1. Contract and invoice, same day. Production dates are reserved on the deposit, not before.
  2. Question set and subject list. Built with you and confirmed before anyone is scheduled.
  3. Interview day. Every voice recorded in one day, in one room, under one lighting setup.
  4. B-roll days. Scheduled against PENCIL events and school days already on your calendar.
  5. First cut, two weeks after the last shoot day. Expedited to one week for $1,200 if the campaign calendar requires it.
  6. Two rounds of notes. One consolidated set of feedback per round, from your single point of contact.
  7. Delivery. Campaign film, social cuts and the b-roll library, delivered together.

Renderings
Supplied by PENCIL at print resolution or better. Static integration is included in every tier. Animated treatment is a Tier Three item.
Confidentiality
If the campaign is pre-launch, all footage, stills, and project details are held confidential. Nothing is published or shown publicly by Laws of Ace Films before PENCIL’s own campaign launch.
Usage
PENCIL receives full unlimited use of all delivered material. Laws of Ace Films retains portfolio rights after the campaign goes public.
Travel
All shoot locations within Davidson County are included.
